The ratio:
In Release with optimization enabled, one million matrix compositions and
conversions take between 51 ms and 61 ms (average 56.3 ms), while the direct
quaternion products take between 19 ms and 23 ms (average 20.3 ms). The
matrix route is therefore about 2.77 times slower than direct quaternions
(56.3 / 20.3 = 2.77). In Debug, the ratio is even higher, taking around 527 ms
versus 117 ms (ratio 4.50).

The cost per frame:
Dividing the Release execution time by one million iterations gives the cost
of a single calculation per frame: approximately 56.3 nanoseconds for the
matrix version and 20.3 nanoseconds for the direct quaternion version. Set
against the total budget of one frame at 90 frames per second (11.11 ms, or
11,111,111 nanoseconds), the matrix calculation represents 0.00051% of the
frame budget. The direct quaternion calculation represents 0.00018% of the
frame budget. The difference between the two is only 36 nanoseconds, which is
0.00032% of the available frame time.

What I think of the choice:
The claim that this calculation happens once per frame for a single head and
costs a few tens of nanoseconds is fully confirmed by the measurements (56 ns
versus 20 ns). The simulator's choice to keep the matrix route followed by a
conversion is completely justified: saving 36 nanoseconds (0.0003% of the
frame budget) would produce no measurable gain in frame rate, while the matrix
path directly aligns with the standard transformation pipeline and camera
matrices of the graphics engine. Prematurely optimizing an isolated 56 ns
calculation would add complexity for zero perceptual benefit.
